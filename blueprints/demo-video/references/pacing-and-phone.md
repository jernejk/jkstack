# Pacing and phone readability

Most of these films are watched on a phone, often in a spare minute. The usual complaint is uneven pacing:
some scenes wait too long and others are too quick. Measure each scene; don't speed up or slow down globally.

## Reading time

- Scene ≥ 1 s entrance + meaningful words ÷ 3 + 1.5 s hold, rounded up to a half bar of the music.
  A four-beat bar lasts `240 / BPM` seconds; find the first real downbeat rather than assuming zero.
- Count the words a viewer must read: headline, labels, numbers, essential UI text, the evidence rail when
  it changes. Over budget: cut words or move the detail to the companion page.
- Takeaway and hero number within 1.5 s of the scene start, visible until the cut.
- Something new every 2–4 s. A held proof frame may be completely still while it is read.
- Don't repeat one treatment in adjacent scenes. Vary the picture, not the colourway.

## Sizes (1920×1080 output)

| Viewing | Scale | 36 px becomes | 80 px becomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phone landscape, full screen (844 px) | 0.44 | 16 px | 35 px |
| Phone portrait, 16:9 player (390 px) | 0.20 | 7 px | 16 px |

- Hero lines ≥ 80 px carry the meaning in portrait; everything that must be read ≥ 36 px works in landscape.
- Source quotes and UI text may be 24–26 px only beside a hero line that states the same fact.
- Text stays inside the 80% title-safe box; the rail inside the 90% action-safe box.
- For a portrait-first destination, recompose a 9:16 cut. Never centre-crop.

## Check it

Make two phone sheets from the render: each scene's midpoint scaled to 844 px and to 390 px wide. Read
both. If a line meant to be read is texture at 844 px, the scene fails. At 390 px only hero lines are
readable, so each scene's meaning and evidence class must survive in its hero line. If the brief names
portrait as the main viewing width, make a portrait composition with larger text instead.
